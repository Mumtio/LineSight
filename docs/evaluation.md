# Evaluation

Method first, then results. Every number in the README table is generated from
a CSV in `results/`, and every CSV is written by a named probe or study, so any
figure traces back to the code that produced it.

> **Status: the reproduction and the fabric studies have run; the remaining
> tables are study design.** Where a result column is empty, the study has not
> been run — nothing here is a placeholder for a number that is expected, and
> where a hypothesis is stated it is labelled as one.

## Metrics

| Metric | What it measures | Why it is here |
|---|---|---|
| **Image AUROC** | Ranking defective images above clean ones | The number published PatchCore results are quoted in, so the only one that makes reproduction checkable. |
| **Pixel AUPRO** | Per-region overlap of the score map with the ground-truth mask | Localisation, not just detection. A model that flags the right image for the wrong reason scores well on AUROC and badly here. |
| **FA per 100 m** | False alarms on defect-free fabric at the chosen threshold | The operational number — the one an operator feels, and the one the threshold is derived from. |
| **ms per tile** | Wall-clock, backbone + NN search | Published with the frame sampling factor, always. |

AUROC and AUPRO are threshold-free, which is what makes them the right metrics
for comparing *scorers*. The false-alarm rate is what matters once a threshold
exists, and it comes from a stated budget rather than from tuning (ADR-007).

## Study design

### 1. Reproduction — MVTec `carpet`

Fit PatchCore on the defect-free train split, evaluate on test. Report image
AUROC and pixel AUPRO against the published number.

This runs first because everything else depends on it. Our PatchCore is
hand-written (ADR-001) and therefore unvalidated; landing near the published
figure is what licenses every subsequent result to be believed. Landing far from
it means there is a bug, and no later table is worth producing until it is found.

| Category | Configuration | Image AUROC (ours) | Pixel AUROC (ours) | Published |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| carpet | product — 30 images, 10% greedy | **0.9615** | 0.9857 | ~0.98 |
| leather | product — 30 images, 10% greedy | **0.9840** | 0.9890 | ~1.00 |
| grid | product — 30 images, 10% greedy | **0.5522** | 0.8566 | ~0.98 |
| grid | product, backbone input 512 | **0.8062** | — | ~0.98 |

**Ran 2026-09-02. Two of three textures reproduce; the third localises a real
limit.** carpet and leather land where the literature does, in the *shipped*
30-image cold-start configuration, using resnet18 where the published figures
use WideResNet-50.

This licenses the rest of the table to be believed, and it settles a question
the fabric results could not: **the detector is correct.** A separation of 1.16×
on Fabric Stain is therefore a property of that data — unrelated photographs
standing in for a roll — and not of the 250 lines.

### grid, and what it costs us

`grid` fails at the shipped configuration: 0.5522, barely above chance. It is
not a bug, it is a resolution limit, and the diagnostic is unambiguous — only
the backbone input matters:

| Working size | Backbone input | Patch grid | Image AUROC |
|---|---|---|---|
| 512 | 320 (shipped) | 20×20 | 0.5522 |
| 512 | **512** | 32×32 | **0.8062** |
| 1024 | 512 | 32×32 | 0.7786 |
| 512 | 320, `grid_align=finest` | 40×40 | 0.6224 |

Reproduce with `--category grid --input-size 512`.

`input_size: 320` was chosen for CPU latency, and it is the right
default for woven cloth — carpet, leather and AITEX are stochastic textures
whose statistics survive downsampling. `grid` is a sparse regular lattice whose
defects are geometric (bent and broken wires), and at a 16 px stride over a
320 px input there are not enough patches to see them. Feeding the backbone
512 px recovers 25 AUROC points; going finer on the *source* image does not,
and neither does aligning to a finer feature grid.

The honest reading: **this system is tuned for textured cloth and degrades on
sparse regular structures.** `input_size` is a per-SKU config key, so the remedy
is available where the latency budget allows it, and the trade is stated rather
than discovered.

→ `results/reproduction.csv`, generated by `probes/p11_mvtec_reproduction.py`

### 5. Cross-construction generalisation on AITEX

**Ran 2026-09-02.** Six fabric codes with enough clean strips and annotated
defects (05 has one defective image, 08 has no clean ones; both are excluded and
the exclusion is stated rather than silent). 256 px tiles, padding cropped, 30
fit tiles per fabric, clean-test tiles disjoint from the fit set, and a tile
counts as defective only where the annotation actually lands in it.

**(a) Cold start — one bank per fabric, detecting that fabric's own defects.**
This is the product's actual operating mode.

| Fabric | Tile AUROC | Pixel AUROC | Defect tiles |
|---|---|---|---|
| 00 | 0.9155 | 0.9513 | 7 |
| 01 | 0.9785 | 0.9796 | 26 |
| 02 | 0.8085 | 0.9803 | 41 |
| 03 | 0.9386 | 0.9670 | 86 |
| 04 | 0.7311 | 0.9963 | 11 |
| 06 | 0.7806 | 0.9514 | 6 |
| **mean** | **0.8588** | **0.9710** | |

**(b) Transfer — a bank fitted on a *different* fabric, same evaluation set.**
30 off-diagonal pairs.

| | Tile AUROC | Pixel AUROC |
|---|---|---|
| **mean** | **0.6290** | **0.7386** |

**In-distribution advantage: +0.230 tile AUROC, +0.232 pixel AUROC.**

That gap is the empirical argument for the whole per-SKU design. A bank carried
over from another construction retains some power — 0.63 is above chance — but
loses roughly a quarter of its discriminative ability. Ninety seconds of
refitting on the mill's own cloth is what buys it back. Had the gap been small,
the per-SKU story would have been unnecessary and should have been dropped;
it is not.

Note also the shape of (a): pixel AUROC (0.971) is far above tile AUROC (0.859),
the same pattern as TFD (block 0.914 against image 0.761). **Localisation is
consistently the strong half and whole-tile detection the weak half**, across
three independent fabric datasets. That is a property of the method, not of one
dataset, and it is what the ASTM pipeline actually needs — the score map lands
on the defect, which is what gets measured and scored.

→ `results/aitex_generalisation.csv`, from `probes/p12_aitex_generalisation.py`

#### A note on `datasets.aitex.lofo_splits`

That helper pairs a bank fitted on fabric X with *other* fabrics' defective
images. Scored against fabric X's own clean tiles as negatives, the resulting
AUROC measures "is this a different fabric" far more strongly than "is this
defective" — every tile of fabric Y is unlike fabric X, defect or not. The
number would look excellent and mean nothing. The study above therefore keeps
clean and defective tiles from the *same* fabric on both sides of every
comparison, so the only thing separating them is the defect. `lofo_splits` is
left in place because it is still the right primitive for grouping by fabric
code; it is the pairing, not the grouping, that needs care.

## Threats to validity

Stated here rather than waited for.

**This PatchCore implementation is not independently validated.** The MVTec
reproduction above is the mitigation, and it is why it runs before anything
else: a fabric result means nothing until the detector is known to be correct
on a benchmark with a published answer.

**AITEX is scanned imagery, not phone imagery.** Its defects are cleaner and its
illumination flatter than a bench rig's. Fabric Stain covers the resolution and
aspect ratio; nothing local covers both at once. The bench-rig runs in
`results/` are the only true in-domain evidence, and there is not much of
it.

**LOFO on 7 fabric structures is a small study.** Seven means differ by more than
their error bars would like. Report the spread, not just the mean.

**The calibration guarantee is exchangeability-based.** It holds if held-out clean
tiles are exchangeable with the tiles seen at run time. A lighting change, a
different operator, or a different fabric lot breaks that, and the threshold must
be recomputed. `Calibration` carries its SKU and timestamp so a stale one is
visible rather than silent.

**Every AUROC here is computed on tiles, not on rolls.** A roll-level false-alarm
rate depends on tile count and overlap, which is exactly why the false-alarm
budget is expressed per 100 metres and not per tile.
