# Evaluation

Method first, then results. Every number in the README table is generated from
a CSV in `results/`, and every CSV is written by a named probe or study, so any
figure traces back to the code that produced it.

> **Status: every study in this document has now run** (sections 2, 4, 6 and 7
> on 2026-09-04; the rest earlier). Two of them contradicted the specification
> they were designed to confirm — the backbone ablation in section 2 and the
> supervised baseline in section 6 — and both are reported as they came out.
> Section 3 remains partially measured: the greedy-vs-random comparison ran,
> the full coreset-fraction sweep did not.

## Metrics

| Metric | What it measures | Why it is here |
|---|---|---|
| **Image AUROC** | Ranking defective images above clean ones | The number published PatchCore results are quoted in, so the only one that makes reproduction checkable. |
| **Pixel AUPRO** | Per-region overlap of the score map with the ground-truth mask | Localisation, not just detection. A model that flags the right image for the wrong reason scores well on AUROC and badly here. |
| **FA per 100 m** | False alarms on defect-free fabric at the chosen threshold | The operational number — the one an operator feels, and the one the threshold is derived from. |
| **ms per tile** | Wall-clock, backbone + NN search | Published with the frame sampling factor, always. |

AUROC and AUPRO are threshold-free, which is what makes them the right metrics
for comparing *scorers*. The false-alarm rate is what matters once a threshold
exists, and it comes from a stated budget rather than from tuning (ADR-007).

## Study design

### 1. Reproduction — MVTec `carpet`

Fit PatchCore on the defect-free train split, evaluate on test. Report image
AUROC and pixel AUPRO against the published number.

This runs first because everything else depends on it. Our PatchCore is
hand-written (ADR-001) and therefore unvalidated; landing near the published
figure is what licenses every subsequent result to be believed. Landing far from
it means there is a bug, and no later table is worth producing until it is found.

| Category | Configuration | Image AUROC (ours) | Pixel AUROC (ours) | Published |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| carpet | product — 30 images, 10% greedy | **0.9615** | 0.9857 | ~0.98 |
| leather | product — 30 images, 10% greedy | **0.9840** | 0.9890 | ~1.00 |
| grid | product — 30 images, 10% greedy | **0.5522** | 0.8566 | ~0.98 |
| grid | product, backbone input 512 | **0.8062** | — | ~0.98 |

**Ran 2026-09-02. Two of three textures reproduce; the third localises a real
limit.** carpet and leather land where the literature does, in the *shipped*
30-image cold-start configuration, using resnet18 where the published figures
use WideResNet-50.

This licenses the rest of the table to be believed, and it settles a question
the fabric results could not: **the detector is correct.** A separation of 1.16×
on Fabric Stain is therefore a property of that data — unrelated photographs
standing in for a roll — and not of the 250 lines.

### grid, and what it costs us

`grid` fails at the shipped configuration: 0.5522, barely above chance. It is
not a bug, it is a resolution limit, and the diagnostic is unambiguous — only
the backbone input matters:

| Working size | Backbone input | Patch grid | Image AUROC |
|---|---|---|---|
| 512 | 320 (shipped) | 20×20 | 0.5522 |
| 512 | **512** | 32×32 | **0.8062** |
| 1024 | 512 | 32×32 | 0.7786 |
| 512 | 320, `grid_align=finest` | 40×40 | 0.6224 |

Reproduce with `--category grid --input-size 512`.

`input_size: 320` was chosen for CPU latency, and it is the right
default for woven cloth — carpet, leather and AITEX are stochastic textures
whose statistics survive downsampling. `grid` is a sparse regular lattice whose
defects are geometric (bent and broken wires), and at a 16 px stride over a
320 px input there are not enough patches to see them. Feeding the backbone
512 px recovers 25 AUROC points; going finer on the *source* image does not,
and neither does aligning to a finer feature grid.

The honest reading: **this system is tuned for textured cloth and degrades on
sparse regular structures.** `input_size` is a per-SKU config key, so the remedy
is available where the latency budget allows it, and the trade is stated rather
than discovered.

→ `results/reproduction.csv`, generated by `probes/p11_mvtec_reproduction.py`

### 2. Backbone ablation

`resnet18` vs `wide_resnet50_2`: accuracy against embedding dimension against
wall-clock per tile. Justifies shipping `resnet18` — or overturns it. The
specification says resnet18 for speed; if the accuracy gap is large this table is
where we find out and change our minds.

**Ran 2026-09-04**, on AITEX rather than MVTec: six fabric codes, one bank per
fabric on its own 30 defect-free tiles, evaluated on that fabric's own defects.
Mean over the six. Accuracy is device-independent; the millisecond column is
measured **on CPU** because that is the target the choice was made for.

| Backbone | Embed dim | Tile AUROC | Pixel AUROC | ms/tile (CPU) | Bank size |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| resnet18 (shipped) | 384 | 0.8588 | 0.9710 | 37 | 1200 |
| wide_resnet50_2 | 1536 | **0.9781** | **0.9831** | 176 | 1200 |

**This overturns the specification's reasoning, and the decision rule was stated
before the run.** p15 recorded in advance that a gap above ~0.05 tile AUROC would
mean resnet18 should be reconsidered. The gap is **+0.1193** — more than
twice that — for 4.8x the CPU cost per tile. The cheap backbone is not free,
and the honest summary is that `resnet18` buys latency at a real accuracy price
rather than at a negligible one.

Two things stop this from being an immediate change of default. The per-tile
cost is what ADR-009's whole latency extrapolation rests on, and `backbone` is
already a per-SKU config key — so a mill with headroom can raise it today
without a code change. What should change is the *claim*: the specification said
resnet18 costs little, and on this evidence it costs 0.12 tile AUROC.

→ `results/backbone_ablation.csv`

### 3. Coreset ablation

1 / 5 / 10 / 25 / 100 % → AUROC and latency, plus greedy against random
selection. Justifies the 10% of ADR-010, which deviates from the paper's 1%
because our fit set is 30 images rather than hundreds.

| Coreset % | Method | Bank size | Image AUROC | ms/tile | Fit time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | greedy | — | — | — | — |
| 5 | greedy | — | — | — | — |
| 10 | greedy | 1200 | **0.9615** | — | 3.6 s |
| 25 | greedy | — | — | — | — |
| 100 | none | — | — | — | — |
| 10 | random | 1200 | **0.9254** | — | 1.1 s |

The greedy/random pair is measured (carpet, 30 images, identical bank size, only
the selection differs): **greedy is worth 3.6 AUROC points** and costs 2.5 s.
That prices ADR-010's documented fallback rather than assuming it, and it is why
greedy stays the default below the ~50-tile point where it becomes quadratic.

→ `results/coreset_ablation.csv`

### 4. Normal-set size sweep — **the money plot**

5 / 10 / 20 / 30 / 50 defect-free images → AUROC.

This is the single highest-return study in this plan. The whitepaper asserts
that a mill can onboard a new SKU from a handful of defect-free samples;
objective O4 puts a number on it. This measures it. If the curve flattens near 20
images, the central claim of the project stops being an assertion and becomes an
observation with an error bar.

If it does *not* flatten, that is a finding too, and it changes the product
claim rather than being quietly dropped.

**Ran 2026-09-04.** The unit is **tiles**, not images: `detect.n_normal_tiles`
is what the product actually parameterises, and one 4096x256 AITEX strip yields
16 tiles, so a sweep in images would move in 16-tile steps and say nothing about
the setting an operator can change. Fit sets are nested (the 5-tile set is a
subset of the 10-tile set) and the evaluation set is identical at every n, so
the only thing moving along the x-axis is the fit size. Mean over six fabrics.

| n tiles | Tile AUROC | sd across fabrics | Pixel AUROC | Bank |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 0.8242 | 0.1053 | 0.9380 | 200 |
| 10 | 0.8092 | 0.1092 | 0.9349 | 400 |
| 20 | 0.8439 | 0.0868 | 0.9558 | 800 |
| 30 | 0.8563 | 0.0887 | 0.9709 | 1200 |
| 50 | 0.8846 | 0.0930 | 0.9740 | 2000 |
| 100 | 0.8937 | 0.0732 | 0.9763 | 4000 |

**The curve does not flatten by 30, and the honest reading is that the claim as
written is too strong.** Going from 5 to 100 tiles moves tile AUROC by
+0.0695; the shipped 30 captures 46% of that. There is no knee — the
gain from 30 to 100 (+0.0374) is roughly the same size as the gain from 5 to 30
(+0.0321).

The caveat that keeps this from being a verdict: the spread across fabrics
(sd ~0.09) is larger than every step in the curve. Six fabrics cannot separate
these means, so the correct statement is "no flattening was observed by 100
tiles", not "the curve is still climbing". What it does establish is that
**30 tiles is a working point, not an optimum**, and the whitepaper should say
that rather than implying the curve has levelled off.

→ `results/normal_set_sweep.csv`

### 5. Cross-construction generalisation on AITEX

**Ran 2026-09-02.** Six fabric codes with enough clean strips and annotated
defects (05 has one defective image, 08 has no clean ones; both are excluded and
the exclusion is stated rather than silent). 256 px tiles, padding cropped, 30
fit tiles per fabric, clean-test tiles disjoint from the fit set, and a tile
counts as defective only where the annotation actually lands in it.

**(a) Cold start — one bank per fabric, detecting that fabric's own defects.**
This is the product's actual operating mode.

| Fabric | Tile AUROC | Pixel AUROC | Defect tiles |
|---|---|---|---|
| 00 | 0.9155 | 0.9513 | 7 |
| 01 | 0.9785 | 0.9796 | 26 |
| 02 | 0.8085 | 0.9803 | 41 |
| 03 | 0.9386 | 0.9670 | 86 |
| 04 | 0.7311 | 0.9963 | 11 |
| 06 | 0.7806 | 0.9514 | 6 |
| **mean** | **0.8588** | **0.9710** | |

**(b) Transfer — a bank fitted on a *different* fabric, same evaluation set.**
30 off-diagonal pairs.

| | Tile AUROC | Pixel AUROC |
|---|---|---|
| **mean** | **0.6290** | **0.7386** |

**In-distribution advantage: +0.230 tile AUROC, +0.232 pixel AUROC.**

That gap is the empirical argument for the whole per-SKU design. A bank carried
over from another construction retains some power — 0.63 is above chance — but
loses roughly a quarter of its discriminative ability. Ninety seconds of
refitting on the mill's own cloth is what buys it back. Had the gap been small,
the per-SKU story would have been unnecessary and should have been dropped;
it is not.

Note also the shape of (a): pixel AUROC (0.971) is far above tile AUROC (0.859),
the same pattern as TFD (block 0.914 against image 0.761). **Localisation is
consistently the strong half and whole-tile detection the weak half**, across
three independent fabric datasets. That is a property of the method, not of one
dataset, and it is what the ASTM pipeline actually needs — the score map lands
on the defect, which is what gets measured and scored.

→ `results/aitex_generalisation.csv`, from `probes/p12_aitex_generalisation.py`

#### A note on `datasets.aitex.lofo_splits`

That helper pairs a bank fitted on fabric X with *other* fabrics' defective
images. Scored against fabric X's own clean tiles as negatives, the resulting
AUROC measures "is this a different fabric" far more strongly than "is this
defective" — every tile of fabric Y is unlike fabric X, defect or not. The
number would look excellent and mean nothing. The study above therefore keeps
clean and defective tiles from the *same* fabric on both sides of every
comparison, so the only thing separating them is the defect. `lofo_splits` is
left in place because it is still the right primitive for grouping by fabric
code; it is the pairing, not the grouping, that needs care.

### 6. Supervised U-Net baseline

Trained on AITEX's 105 pixel-annotated defective images (ADR-004). Evaluated in
two conditions against PatchCore, using the same harness — it implements the same
`AnomalyScorer` protocol, so the swap is two lines.

**Ran 2026-09-04.** 4 fabrics, ~1.9 M-parameter U-Net, 30 epochs, Dice+BCE,
crops balanced so half contain an annotated defect. All three methods are scored
on **identical** tiles through the identical AUROC path.

| Method | Saw this construction? | Tile AUROC | Pixel AUROC |
|---|---|---|---|
| Supervised U-Net, in-distribution | yes | 0.7726 | 0.7896 |
| Supervised U-Net, leave-one-fabric-out | no | 0.6277 | 0.6957 |
| PatchCore, cold start (shipped) | no defect labels at all | **0.8631** | **0.9841** |

**Hypothesis** (recorded before running, so it cannot be retrofitted): the U-Net
wins in-distribution and collapses on held-out structures; PatchCore sits
slightly behind in-distribution and holds.

**The second half held. The first half did not, and that is reported rather than
quietly reframed.** The U-Net collapsed on unseen constructions exactly as
predicted (-0.1449 tile AUROC, and pixel AUROC falling to 0.70), and
PatchCore beats it there by **+0.2354**. But PatchCore also beat it
*in-distribution*, by 0.0905 tile AUROC and 0.1946 pixel AUROC — so the
"supervised upper bound" framing this study was designed around is wrong on this
data.

**Do not read that as "supervised learning is worse".** The honest reading is
that ~50 training images and 30 epochs is a thin budget, and a bigger corpus or
a longer schedule would likely close the in-distribution gap. What the table
does support is narrower and still decisive for the product argument:

> A supervised segmentation model trained on every pixel-annotated defect we
> have does not beat a method that saw no defect labels at all — and on a fabric
> construction it has never seen, it loses by 0.24 tile AUROC. The
> cold-start case is the one a mill lives in.

The pixel-level gap is the sharper one: 0.9841 against 0.7896. The U-Net
finds roughly the right images and marks the wrong pixels, which is the failure
AUPRO-style metrics exist to expose.

The cold-start argument stands independently of all of this: a labelled corpus
per construction does not exist in a mill, which is what `UNetScorer.fit`
says by raising.

→ `results/baseline_comparison.csv`, `results/unet_history.csv`

### 7. Calibration curve

Threshold against false alarms per 100 m on held-out clean tiles. Verifies
empirically that a stated budget of 1 FA/100 m produces approximately 1 FA/100 m
— that the conformal quantile of ADR-007 does what it claims on this data.

Also reports the achievable resolution at each sample size, which is the guard
that stops the method from quoting a rate the sample cannot support.

**Ran 2026-09-04.** Seven fabrics, ~350 calibration and ~350 validation tiles
each, disjoint from each other and from the 30 fit tiles.

The x-axis is the **allowed tail fraction**, not a rate per 100 m, and that is
deliberate. The conformal guarantee is a statement about a quantile of the
clean-score distribution; turning it into a rate needs `metres_per_tile`, which
is a property of the rig rather than of the method, and AITEX strips carry no
scale. The second column converts using the value this project's own bench rig
measured, and exists only to connect the two.

| Requested tail fraction | = FA/100 m at bench scale | Fabrics resolved | Realised fraction | Realised / requested |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.005 | 33 | 0/7 | refused | — |
| 0.010 | 67 | 0/7 | refused | — |
| 0.020 | 133 | 3/7 | 0.0192 | 0.96 |
| 0.030 | 200 | 7/7 | 0.0270 | 0.90 |
| 0.050 | 333 | 7/7 | 0.0478 | 0.96 |
| 0.075 | 500 | 7/7 | 0.0755 | 1.01 |
| 0.100 | 666 | 7/7 | 0.1030 | 1.03 |
| 0.150 | 999 | 7/7 | 0.1494 | 1.00 |
| 0.200 | 1,332 | 7/7 | 0.1959 | 0.98 |

**Both halves of ADR-007 hold on real fabric.** Where the sample can resolve the
request, the realised exceedance tracks it: mean ratio **0.98**, median
0.97 over 45 resolvable cases, running hot in 21 of 45 — scattered either
side of 1.0 rather than biased, which is what "derived, not chosen" has to mean
to be worth anything. And where the sample cannot support the request, the
function **refuses**: 18 of 63 cases, all 18 of them tripping the
stability-margin guard rather than the cruder existence check.

**The wall is the finding, and it is not flattering.** At bench scale a tail
fraction of 0.03 is already ~200 FA/100 m. Resolving the 1 FA/100 m the
whitepaper quotes needs a tail fraction of 1.5e-4 and, with the default
stability margin of 10, on the order of 70,000 clean tiles — about a kilometre
of clean fabric. AITEX gives a few hundred tiles per fabric. This is the same
arithmetic that made the bench rig calibrate at 40 FA/100 m rather than at 1,
and it means **the 1 FA/100 m figure is a design target the current evidence
cannot yet demonstrate**, not a measured result.

One caveat, stated because it inflates the sample: tiles were cut with 192 px
overlap to raise the count, so neighbouring tiles are correlated and the
effective sample size is smaller than the tile count. That makes the refusal
boundary above *optimistic* — the real one sits at coarser fractions.

→ `results/calibration.csv`

## Threats to validity

Stated here rather than waited for.

**This PatchCore implementation is not independently validated.** The MVTec
reproduction above is the mitigation, and it is why it runs before anything
else: a fabric result means nothing until the detector is known to be correct
on a benchmark with a published answer.

**AITEX is scanned imagery, not phone imagery.** Its defects are cleaner and its
illumination flatter than a bench rig's. Fabric Stain covers the resolution and
aspect ratio; nothing local covers both at once. The bench-rig runs in
`results/` are the only true in-domain evidence, and there is not much of
it.

**LOFO on 7 fabric structures is a small study.** Seven means differ by more than
their error bars would like. Report the spread, not just the mean.

**The calibration guarantee is exchangeability-based.** It holds if held-out clean
tiles are exchangeable with the tiles seen at run time. A lighting change, a
different operator, or a different fabric lot breaks that, and the threshold must
be recomputed. `Calibration` carries its SKU and timestamp so a stale one is
visible rather than silent.

**Every AUROC here is computed on tiles, not on rolls.** A roll-level false-alarm
rate depends on tile count and overlap, which is exactly why the false-alarm
budget is expressed per 100 metres and not per tile.
